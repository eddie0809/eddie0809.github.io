Recipes

2026/11/25

I don't really like recipes. It's not because I don't like cooking, no I love cooking. It's more that recipes are a weird way to place confidence in the exactness of a protocoll within an inexact operational framework. What do I mean by this? In part I mean that the joy of abstraction has not yet come to the field of recipes. If I look up a recipe, most of the time "all" the steps are laid out in the recipe, and I put "all" in quotations since it's not really every step.

Consider the very simple meal of potatoes with spinach. I have been quite fond of that meal recently. I did try and follow a recipe for a creamy spinach sauce, but what I ended up with was a mess of flour clumps. In principle, this is my mistake, I could have been more aware of the intricacies of roux making, but alas, i was not. Does this mean I am mad that my sauce was slightly bad and that's why I hate recipes now? No! I dislike recipes for a reason related to my failure bac there. It is that if the person would have written "roux" or somnething like that, this would likely not have happened in the same way.

This also relates to another problem I have with recipes: The ingredients.
I think it's good that ingredient lists exist, and that they can help me gauge the amount of meal I will have at the end. However, most of the time, I will not go out and buy "one third" pack of cream or something like that. Recipes that I cook for me, or even for me and my girlfriend, will often have ingredients left in a weirdly small fraction of the original portion. That is, What am I to do with 200g of potatoes except buy another pack and do the same recipe again. Or with 50ml of cream for that matter. Then, in the next instance, take Semmelknödel for example. The amount of milk you need for the dough is entirely dependent on the quality and humidity of the bread you're using. Using reallz old bread that can be used to smash in windows? Use more milk (how much more can no-one really say with certainty). Using breadrolls from yesterday that just can't be sold anymore, but would be fine to eat as such still? There you have two options: raw dog that shit, or use less milk.

Yet in the end I find myself creating a recipe section in my own website. So whats the deal?
Long story short, I want to have a place for me to add stuff I found while improvising in the kitchen. Low-budget meals, complicated meals, simple meals, stupid meals, surprising combinations, collection of restaurant-grade recipes (more in the sense of quantity than quality) and recipes friends told me in a conversation that I found to be really good.
